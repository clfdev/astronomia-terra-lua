Earth's Orbit Information

Earth follows an elliptical path around the Sun, although it's an ellipse with low eccentricity (0.0167), very close to a perfect circle.

The equation of Earth's orbit:

r = a(1-e²)/(1+e·cos(θ))

Where:

  • r is the Earth-Sun distance
  • a is the semi-major axis of the orbit (149.6 million km)
  • e is the eccentricity of the orbit (0.0167)
  • θ is the angle in the orbital plane

The distance variation between perihelion and aphelion is approximately 5 million kilometers, which represents about 3.4% of the average Earth-Sun distance.

Characteristics of Earth's orbit:

  • Orbital period (sidereal year): 365.256 days
  • Orbital period (tropical year): 365.242 days
  • Average orbital velocity: 29.78 km/s
  • Velocity at perihelion: 30.29 km/s
  • Velocity at aphelion: 29.29 km/s

Important points in the orbit:

  • Perihelion: closest point to the Sun (January 3)
  • Aphelion: farthest point from the Sun (July 4)
  • Autumn Equinox: March 20 (southern hemisphere)
  • Winter Solstice: June 21 (southern hemisphere)
  • Spring Equinox: September 22 (southern hemisphere)
  • Summer Solstice: December 21 (southern hemisphere)

Earth's axial tilt:

  • Earth's rotation axis is tilted 23.5° relative to the plane of its orbit (the ecliptic plane).
  • This tilt is responsible for the seasons, as it changes the amount of sunlight received at different latitudes throughout the year.
  • During solstices, one hemisphere receives more sunlight than the other.
  • During equinoxes, both hemispheres receive the same amount of light.

Seasons in the southern hemisphere:

  • Summer in Brazil: between the Summer Solstice (Dec 21) and the Autumn Equinox (Mar 20)
  • Autumn in Brazil: between the Autumn Equinox (Mar 20) and the Winter Solstice (Jun 21)
  • Winter in Brazil: between the Winter Solstice (Jun 21) and the Spring Equinox (Sep 22)
  • Spring in Brazil: between the Spring Equinox (Sep 22) and the Summer Solstice (Dec 21)
