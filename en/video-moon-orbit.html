Understanding Moon Phases

The Moon completes one orbit around the Earth in approximately 27.3 days (sidereal month). However, because the Earth is also moving around the Sun, it takes about 29.5 days (synodic month) for the Moon to go through all its phases as seen from Earth.

The phases of the Moon are determined by the relative positions of the Moon, Earth, and Sun. The Moon is always half-illuminated by the Sun, but from Earth, we see different portions of the illuminated half as the Moon orbits around us.

  • New Moon: The illuminated side of the Moon faces the Sun and is not visible from Earth.
  • First Quarter: Half of the visible face of the Moon is illuminated, forming a "D" shape.
  • Full Moon: The visible face of the Moon is fully illuminated.
  • Last Quarter: Half of the visible face is illuminated, forming a "C" shape.
