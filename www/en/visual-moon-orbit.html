Information about Lunar Movement

The Moon follows an elliptical path around Earth, with an eccentricity of 0.0549, significantly greater than the eccentricity of Earth's orbit around the Sun (0.0167).

The equation of lunar motion:
r = a(1-e²)/(1+e·cos(θ))

Where:

  • r is the Earth-Moon distance
  • a is the semi-major axis of the orbit (384,400 km)
  • e is the eccentricity of the orbit (0.0549)
  • θ is the angle in the orbital plane

The distance variation between perigee and apogee is approximately 42,207 km, which represents about 11% of the average Earth-Moon distance.

Orbital periods:

  • Sidereal period: 27.32 days - time for the Moon to complete one orbit relative to the fixed stars.
  • Synodic period: 29.53 days - time between identical lunar phases (complete cycle from New Moon to New Moon).

Moon phases:

  • New Moon: The illuminated face of the Moon is facing the Sun and is not visible from Earth.
  • First Quarter: Half of the visible face of the Moon is illuminated, forming a "D" (northern hemisphere) or an inverted "C" (southern hemisphere).
  • Full Moon: The visible face of the Moon is completely illuminated.
  • Last Quarter: Half of the visible face is illuminated, forming a "C" (northern hemisphere) or an inverted "D" (southern hemisphere).

Additional characteristics:

  • Synchronous rotation: The Moon always shows the same face to Earth because its rotation period equals its orbital period.
  • Orbital inclination: The Moon's orbit has an inclination of approximately 5.14° relative to the ecliptic plane (Earth's orbit around the Sun).
  • This inclination is responsible for eclipses not occurring monthly.
